03

How to build a stronger password

  1. Go long first

    Length matters more than anything else. Every extra character multiplies the number of guesses needed. Aim for 12+ characters, or 16+ for important accounts.

  2. Mix character types

    Using lowercase, uppercase, numbers and symbols grows the "alphabet" an attacker has to search from 26 characters to around 94.

  3. Avoid the predictable

    Names, birthdays, pet names, football teams, 123, qwerty and "password" are the first things attackers try. Swapping a for @ doesn't fool them either.

  4. Try a passphrase

    Pick 4 or more random, unrelated words: Cactus-Velvet-Orbit-Lantern. Easy for you to remember, hard for a computer to guess.

  5. One account, one password

    If one site leaks, reused passwords let attackers walk into your other accounts. A password manager makes unique passwords painless.

04

Everyday security tips

a.

Use a password manager

Apps like Bitwarden, 1Password or your browser's built-in manager remember strong, unique passwords so you don't have to.

b.

Turn on two-factor login

2FA adds a second step, like a code from an app. Even if your password leaks, the attacker still can't get in.

c.

Watch out for phishing

No real company will ask for your password by email or message. Check the website address before you log in.

d.

Never share passwords

Not with friends, not over chat, not on sticky notes on your monitor. Shared passwords get passed around.

e.

Check for leaks

Services such as Have I Been Pwned tell you if your email appeared in a data breach. Change those passwords first.

f.

Lock your devices

Use a PIN, pattern or fingerprint on your phone and laptop, and log out of shared or school computers.

g.

Change after a breach

You don't need to change passwords every month. Change them straight away if a site you use is hacked.

h.

Keep software updated

Updates fix security holes. A strong password can't protect a device running years-old software.
